## The obligation

Under British Columbia's Builders Lien Act, an owner making progress payments on a construction project is required to retain a holdback of 10% of each payment [VERIFY: exact statutory basis — percentage and whether it is calculated on the payment or on the value of the work]. The obligation is statutory. It applies whether or not the construction contract mentions it, and it cannot be contracted away. A homeowner who contracts directly with a general contractor is an "owner" for this purpose [VERIFY: definition of "owner" in the Act as it applies to a private residential owner].

The retained funds are held for a defined period after the work reaches the statutory trigger [VERIFY: holdback period in days — secondary sources disagree between 45 and 55 — and the exact trigger event], during which parties further down the chain — subcontractors, workers, suppliers — can file liens against the project [VERIFY: lien filing deadline]. Only after that period expires, and after confirming what has been filed, is the holdback properly released.

## What it protects you from

The holdback is the mechanism that caps your exposure to claims from people you never contracted with. Comply, and your liability to downstream lien claimants is limited to the holdback pool [VERIFY: the cap provision]. Fail to retain it — pay your contractor in full, in good faith, with every invoice honoured — and you can become personally exposed to lien claims up to the amount that should have been held back [VERIFY: liability provision], on top of everything already paid. Owners discover this obligation most often at the worst possible moment: after a contractor's insolvency, when the subcontractors who were never paid look to the project for their money.

## Why your builder can't do this for you

The holdback exists to protect the owner from the contracting chain — from the consequences of a contractor not paying the parties beneath them. Your builder can be diligent, solvent and honest, and it changes nothing about the structure: **the party being held back from cannot administer the holdback.** A builder who says "we'll take care of the holdback" is offering to hold back money from themselves. However good their intentions, that is not what the Act contemplates, and it is not protection. The obligation is the owner's, and so is the exposure.
