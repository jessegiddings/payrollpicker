## Part 1 — Builders Lien Act

### 1.1 The 10% holdback

| | |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | The person primarily liable on each contract must retain a holdback equal to 10% of the greater of (a) the value of work or material as actually provided, and (b) the amount of any payment made on account of the contract price. |
| **Authority** | Builders Lien Act, s.4(1) |
| **Checked** | 25 Aug 2026 · bclaws |
| **Status** | **CONFIRMED.** Statutory wording tracks the claim, including the "greater of" formulation. |

**Note for drafting.** The duty attaches to "the person primarily liable on each
contract," which is the owner as against the head contractor, and the head
contractor as against each subcontractor. This is the multiple-holdback structure
the 1997 Act introduced. Do not describe the holdback as a single pool held by the
owner covering the whole project.

### 1.2 The obligation is statutory, not contractual

| | |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | The holdback obligation applies whether or not the contract mentions it. |
| **Authority** | Builders Lien Act, s.4(1), s.4(2) |
| **Checked** | 25 Aug 2026 · bclaws |
| **Status** | **AMENDED — restate precisely.** s.4(2) provides that the obligation applies "whether or not the contract or subcontract provides for periodic payments or payment on completion." That addresses payment structure, not contractual silence generally. |

**Approved formulation:** *The duty is imposed by s.4(1) on the person primarily
liable on the contract. It is not a term the parties negotiate into the agreement,
and s.4(2) confirms it applies whether the contract provides for periodic payments
or a single payment on completion.* Do not write "whether or not the contract
mentions it" — that is an inference, not the statutory text.

### 1.3 A private homeowner is an "owner"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | A homeowner contracting with a general contractor is an "owner" under the Act. |
| **Authority** | Builders Lien Act, s.1 definition of "owner" |
| **Checked** | 25 Aug 2026 · bclaws |
| **Status** | **CONFIRMED.** The definition captures a person with a legal or equitable estate or interest in the land at whose request, on whose credit, on whose behalf, with whose knowledge or consent, or for whose direct benefit the work is done. |

### 1.4 The holdback account and the $100,000 threshold

| | |
|---|---|
| **Claim as received** | Where the prime contract exceeds $100,000, the owner must establish a holdback trust account at a savings institution, jointly administered with the contractor. |
| **Authority** | Builders Lien Act, s.5(1)(a)–(c), s.5(8)(b) |
| **Checked** | 25 Aug 2026 · bclaws |
| **Status** | **AMENDED — the threshold is expressed as an exemption, and the test is not "prime contract price."** |

s.5(1) requires the owner to (a) establish a holdback account at a savings
institution for each contract under which a lien may arise, (b) pay into it the
amount required to be retained under s.4, and (c) administer it **together with
the contractor from whom the holdback was retained**.

s.5(8) then disapplies s.5 to: (a) the government, a government corporation, or
a prescribed public body; and (b) "a contract in respect of an improvement, if the
aggregate value of work and material provided is less than $100 000."

**Approved formulation:** *The account requirement does not apply where the
aggregate value of work and material provided under the contract is less than
$100,000 (s.5(8)(b)).* Note the two consequences: the test is aggregate value of
work and material, not the headline contract price; and a contract that starts
below the threshold and grows past it moves into the requirement.

**Also confirmed and worth using:** s.5(7) — failure by the owner to comply with
s.5(1)(b) is an act of default under the contract, and the contractor may, on 10
days' notice, suspend operations for as long as the default continues. s.5(6) —
interest on the account accrues to the owner during the holdback period, and to
the contractor's credit after it.

### 1.5 What non-compliance actually exposes an owner to

| | |
|---|---|
| **Claim as received** | An owner who pays in full without retaining holdback becomes personally liable for lien claims up to the amount that should have been retained; an owner who complies caps exposure at the holdback pool. |
| **Authority** | Builders Lien Act, s.34(1); s.34(2)(c) |
| **Checked** | 25 Aug 2026 · bclaws |
| **Status** | **AMENDED — the mechanism is a recovery cap, and the cap is not always the holdback.** |

s.34(1): the maximum aggregate amount recoverable under the Act by all lien
holders claiming under the same contractor or subcontractor is **the greater of**
(a) the amount owing to that contractor or subcontractor by the person who engaged
them, and (b) the amount of the required holdback for that contract.

Two consequences, both of which the content must state correctly:

1. Because the cap is the **greater** of the two, an owner who has paid the
   contractor in full and retained nothing is still exposed up to the amount of the
   holdback that should have been retained. The money has already gone out the
   door; the exposure remains. That is the "paying twice" outcome, and it is the
   correct centre of gravity for Chapter 5 and article #1.
2. Compliance does **not** always cap exposure at 10%. Where the amount still
   owing to the contractor exceeds the required holdback — the ordinary position
   mid-project — the cap is the amount owing. What compliance buys is that the
   money needed to satisfy the cap is money the owner has not yet paid out.

**Do not write** "personally liable" as though the Act creates a personal debt.
The Act creates liens against the land and a statutory cap on recovery. Write the
consequence — the owner funds it, and may fund it twice — not a characterisation
of the liability.

**Also confirmed:** s.34(2)(c) — a payment made to a contractor after a claim of
lien has been filed, where the payer has actual notice and the lien has not been
removed, does not reduce the amount owing for the purpose of the cap.

### 1.6 The 55-day holdback period — and the 45-day discrepancy

| | |
|---|---|
| **Claim** | Holdback period is 55 days from the earliest of a certificate of completion being issued, or head contract completion / abandonment / termination. Secondary sources variously said 45 days. |
| **Authority** | Builders Lien Act, s.8(1), s.8(2); s.20 |
| **Checked** | 25 Aug 2026 · bclaws |
| **Status** | **CONFIRMED, and the discrepancy is resolved.** |

Both numbers are real and they govern different things:

- **s.8** — the **holdback period** expires 55 days after a certificate of
  completion is issued (s.8(1)); or, absent one, 55 days after the head contract
  is completed, abandoned or terminated, or the improvement is completed or
  abandoned (s.8(2)).
- **s.20** — a **claim of lien** must be filed no later than **45 days** after the
  corresponding event.

The 45 days sits inside the 55. Content must never use the two interchangeably,
and must name the section each time.

**Also confirmed:** s.8(4) — payment of the holdback may be made after expiry of
the holdback period, and liens of the person paid and those engaged under them are
then discharged, unless a claim of lien has in the meantime been filed or
proceedings commenced.

### 1.9 The trust fund — a correction to the brief

| | |
|---|---|
| **Claim as received** | "s.8 (trust account)" — implying the owner holds statutory trust money. |
| **Authority** | Builders Lien Act, s.10(1) |
| **Checked** | 25 Aug 2026 · bclaws |
| **Status** | **AMENDED — the section reference was wrong and so was the party.** |

s.10(1) makes money **received by a contractor or subcontractor** on account of the
contract price a trust fund for the benefit of persons they engaged, with the
contractor or subcontractor as trustee. **The trust binds the contractor, not the
owner.** The owner's parallel obligation is the s.5 holdback account, which is a
different mechanism. s.8 is the holdback period. Content must not conflate the
two, and must not tell owners they are statutory trustees.

### 1.10 Title search before releasing holdback

| | |
|---|---|
| **Claim as received** | Before release, conduct an LTSA title search to confirm no liens registered. |
| **Authority** | None found in the Act |
| **Checked** | 25 Aug 2026 · bclaws |
| **Status** | **AMENDED — this is prudent practice, not a statutory requirement.** |

The Act contains no express requirement that an owner search title before paying
out holdback. The protective structure is s.8(4): payment after expiry discharges
liens **unless** a claim has been filed in the meantime. The title search is how a
careful owner finds out whether that "unless" has been triggered. Content must
present it as operational practice, and must not imply the statute compels it.

## Part 5 — Departures from the content brief, for the record

Two instructions in the content handoff could not be followed as written, because
the primary sources did not support them.

**1. The referral kit's specified sentence.** The handoff specifies a sentence for
referrers: *"They make sure you don't pay for work that isn't there, and that you
don't end up personally liable for your contractor's unpaid trades."* Two problems.
"Personally liable" mischaracterises the Act — s.34 creates liens against the land
and a statutory cap on recovery, not a personal debt (§1.5). And "make sure" is an
outcome guarantee. The sentence as published instead reads:

> *"They check that the work exists before your client pays for it — and they keep
> the holdback the Builders Lien Act requires, so your client isn't left funding
> their contractor's unpaid trades out of money they have already paid."*

Longer, and accurate. **This is a deliberate departure from the brief and should be
confirmed by the partners.**

**2. "Personally liable" in article #1's brief.** The brief directs article #1 to
"lead with the personal-liability consequence." The article leads with the
consequence — an owner paying twice — without characterising it as personal
liability, for the reason above.
